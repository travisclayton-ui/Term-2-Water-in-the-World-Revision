"India accounts for 18% of the world's population but has access to only about 4% of the world's water resources. Most rainfall occurs during the monsoon season, from June to September, with very little rain falling in other months. Safe drinking water and adequate water for irrigation remains a persistent challenge. As of 2015, only 78% of rural Indians and 85% of urban Indians had access to potable (drinkable) water. Section 7 — WaterAid: WASH GAINS Project, Nepal

Read the project description, then answer Questions 27–30.

About WaterAid's WASH GAINS Project — Bardiya District, Nepal

WaterAid is a non-government organisation (NGO) working in Nepal to improve access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). Their WASH GAINS project runs from 2023 to 2026 in Bardiya District in midwestern Nepal. In this area, only 70.5% of girls enrol in secondary school, and many drop out when they begin menstruating because school toilets are not private, clean, or suitable for managing periods. Girls from marginalised communities — such as those with disabilities — face even greater barriers.

The project aims to reach around 25,000 students and teachers across 30 schools. Activities include: installing new, gender-inclusive and disability-friendly toilets; providing hygiene education to 13,000 students; running media campaigns to break cultural taboos around menstruation; and training families in income generation to reduce poverty — a key driver of school dropout.

Wider benefits include improved public health (fewer waterborne diseases), better environmental outcomes from reduced open defecation, and stronger school attendance for girls — linking water access to education, gender equality and economic development.

Source: WaterAid WASH GAINS project, washmatters.wateraid.org; WaterAid Nepal Country Programme Strategy 2023–28.
